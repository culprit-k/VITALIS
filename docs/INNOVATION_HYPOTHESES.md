# Research Hypotheses (Not Claims of Novelty)

These are hypotheses to test, not assertions that the method is novel, patentable, or clinically useful.

1. Patient-relative deviation from a patient's own recent baseline may add predictive information beyond absolute measurements.
2. Short-term slopes and variability may improve prediction over single-time-point values.
3. Measurement frequency may reflect clinical workflow and introduce bias; test this explicitly rather than assuming it is physiology.
4. Model utility depends on alert burden and event-level sensitivity, not discrimination alone.
5. Performance and calibration may differ across demographic and care-setting subgroups.

## Experimental discipline
Predefine the comparison, features, split strategy, metrics, and stopping criteria. Compare against transparent baselines. Perform patient-level validation and report uncertainty. Any future patent or novelty assessment requires a separate prior-art and professional review.
