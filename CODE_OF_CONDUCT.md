# Code of Conduct

## Our commitment

The VITALIS project welcomes participation from people with different backgrounds, disciplines, experience levels, and perspectives. We are committed to a respectful, evidence-led environment where people can ask questions, disagree, identify limitations, and improve the work together.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful, constructive, and considerate.
- Critique ideas, methods, evidence, and code—not a person's identity or worth.
- Explain technical disagreements with reasons, references, reproducible examples, or clearly stated uncertainty.
- Welcome beginners and make room for different professional and cultural perspectives.
- Disclose relevant conflicts of interest when providing scientific or product-related recommendations.
- Respect privacy, confidentiality, intellectual property, and research-governance requirements.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, discrimination, intimidation, personal attacks, or targeted insults.
- Publishing private information without consent.
- Repeated unwanted contact or deliberately disrupting discussions.
- Misrepresenting credentials, validation status, results, or another person's contribution.
- Sharing identifiable patient information, credentials, or confidential research material.
- Promoting unsafe patient-connected hardware experiments or clinical use of this unvalidated prototype.

## Scope

This code applies to project issues, pull requests, code reviews, documentation, project communication channels, and events explicitly organised for VITALIS.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report conduct concerns privately to the repository maintainer through the contact options on the maintainer's GitHub profile. Do not include private or sensitive details in a public issue.

The maintainer will review reports fairly and, where appropriate, may edit or remove content, issue a warning, temporarily restrict participation, or block a participant. Decisions should be proportionate to the behaviour and take account of safety and privacy risks.

## A shared standard

Scientific criticism is welcome, including strong disagreement. It must remain focused on the evidence and be communicated respectfully.
